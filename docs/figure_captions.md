# Figure Captions

## Main Text

**Figure 1.** Model-Free Evidence of Politically Polarized Brand-to-Generic Switching During the Health Crisis.
(*a*) Change in brand-name drug market share (percentage points) from April–June 2019 to April–June 2020, by political group. Republican-leaning counties experienced a 1.34 percentage-point decline in brand market share, nearly seven times the 0.20-point decline in Democrat-leaning counties. Error bars indicate 95% confidence intervals. (*b*) Percentage change in transaction volume by political group and drug type. In Republican-leaning counties, brand transactions fell by 46.5% compared with 39.0% for generics, indicating differential switching. In Democrat-leaning counties, declines were nearly identical for brand (47.3%) and generic (46.2%) products. Counties classified into terciles of 2020 Republican two-party vote share. N = 96,224 G03 transactions across 122 counties in 8 southeastern U.S. states.

**Figure 2.** Distribution of Individual Treatment Effects by Brand Status.
Kernel density estimates of causal forest individual treatment effects (ITEs) for (*A*) log(Sales), (*B*) log(Average Price), and (*C*) log(Transactions), separated by brand-name (red) and generic (blue) products. Dashed vertical lines indicate group means. Across all three outcomes, brand-name products exhibit a leftward shift relative to generics, consistent with the BLP interaction reported in Table 1. The near-complete overlap in panel C confirms that transaction volumes did not differentially change, supporting brand-to-generic switching rather than demand suppression. Distributions trimmed to the central 90% of observations. N = 22,737 county-day observations.

**Figure 3.** Within-G03 Subcategory Gradient: BLP Interaction Coefficients on log(Sales).
Coefficient plot showing the BLP interaction (Republican vote share × brand status) for three G03 subcategories. Horizontal bars indicate 95% confidence intervals. The gradient aligns with predictions from the identity concealment framework: the strongest effect for estrogens (β = −0.038, p = 0.089), an identity-contested subcategory dispensed at the pharmacy level; a weaker effect for contraceptives (β = −0.027, p = 0.125), similarly dispensed but with out-of-pocket costs substantially reduced by the ACA contraceptive mandate; and a precise null for androgens (β = +0.035, p = 0.659), where physician-controlled prescribing removes patient substitution autonomy. Progestins omitted due to insufficient brand variation.

## Supplement

**Parallel Trends Test.** Brand Drug Share by Political Ideology, 2019.
(*A*) Time series of brand drug market share for high- and low-Republican counties during the pre-treatment period (April–June 2019). (*B*) Weekly difference in brand share between high- and low-Republican counties. The pre-treatment slope is −0.001 (p = 0.330), confirming that brand drug shares evolved in parallel across political groups before the pandemic onset.

**Between-Category Placebo.** BLP Interaction Coefficients on log(Sales).
Coefficient plot comparing the BLP interaction (Republican vote share × brand status) across four drug categories. Sex hormones (G03) show a significant negative interaction (β = −0.034, p = 0.044), while all three placebo categories, lipid-modifying agents (C10, p = 0.402), cardiac therapy (C01, p = 0.899), and antihypertensives (C02, p = 0.786), are centered near zero with wide confidence intervals. This confirms that the effect is specific to politically salient sex hormones.

**Overall ITE Distributions.** Distribution of Individual Treatment Effects from Causal Forest.
Overall ITE distributions (not separated by brand status) for (*A*) log(Sales), (*B*) log(Average Price), and (*C*) log(Transactions). Black curves show kernel density estimates; red vertical lines indicate the average treatment effect (ATE); gray shaded regions indicate 95% confidence intervals. The distributions confirm substantial treatment effect heterogeneity across all three outcomes. N = 22,737.

**ITE by Republican Tercile.** Individual Treatment Effects by Republican Vote Share Tercile.
ITE distributions for (*A*) log(Sales), (*B*) log(Average Price), and (*C*) log(Transactions), separated by county-level Republican vote share tercile (low, medium, high). Higher-Republican counties show a more negative ITE distribution for sales and prices, consistent with the BLP interaction. N = 22,737.
