**Data and sample.** We use county-level daily pharmaceutical claims data covering 8 southeastern U.S. states (Alabama, Florida, Georgia, Kentucky, Mississippi, North Carolina, South Carolina, and Tennessee) from April through June in 2019 and 2020. The unit of analysis is a county-day-product type observation, where product type distinguishes brand-name from generic drugs within ATC category G03 (sex hormones and modulators of the genital system). The final sample comprises 22,737 observations across 122 counties.

We construct three outcome variables to enable mechanism decomposition: total sales revenue (log(1 + sales)), average unit price (log(1 + avg price)), and transaction count (log(1 + transactions)). The treatment variable is a binary indicator for April–June 2020 versus 2019. Because treatment assignment is determined entirely by calendar time, it is exogenous and identical across all units within each period. County-level political affiliation is measured by standardized Republican two-party vote share in the 2020 presidential election. We interact this measure with a brand-name drug indicator to construct our primary variable of interest, which captures whether political conservatism differentially affects brand-name versus generic purchasing during the health crisis.

**Causal forest.** We employ a Generalized Random Forest (GRF) framework (12, 13) to estimate heterogeneous treatment effects. Causal forests partition the feature space through recursive splitting to estimate individual-level conditional average treatment effects (CATEs) that vary non-parametrically across units, following approaches for heterogeneous treatment effects in observational settings (30). We estimate a forest with 3,000 trees and minimum node size 15, with standard errors clustered at the county level. Because treatment is deterministic, we set the propensity score to the known sample treatment proportion for all observations, following the recommendation in Athey and Wager (31) for settings where the assignment mechanism is known.

The forest is trained on 7 heterogeneity features (standardized Republican vote share, brand indicator, their interaction, and four drug subcategory volume indicators) and 13 control variables spanning prescription volume, healthcare access, demographics, socioeconomic status, cultural composition, and temporal trends (SI Appendix, Table A.1). Because treatment is deterministic, controls serve to improve estimation precision rather than to address confounding; the interaction coefficient remains stable at β ≈ −0.034 across specifications ranging from 5 to 45 controls (SI Appendix, Section D).

**Best Linear Projection.** To formally test whether treatment effects vary with political affiliation, we apply the Best Linear Projection (BLP) method (14). BLP projects the estimated CATEs onto covariates using doubly-robust augmented inverse-propensity weighted (AIPW) scores derived from the causal forest, providing asymptotically valid inference even when the forest is used as a first-stage estimator. We regress the AIPW scores on standardized Republican vote share and its interaction with the brand indicator; the interaction coefficient is our primary quantity of interest. Standard errors are clustered at the county level.

**Between-category placebo tests.** We apply the identical GRF+BLP procedure to three politically neutral ATC drug categories, C10 (lipid-modifying agents), C01 (cardiac therapy), and C02 (antihypertensives), that lack the political salience of sex hormones. For these categories, which have no drug subcategory indicators, heterogeneity features are reduced to 3; the 13 controls remain unchanged.

**Within-G03 subcategory analysis.** We separately analyze four G03 subcategories (androgens/anabolics, estrogens, progestins, contraceptives) by restricting the sample to observations with positive dispensing volume. Our framework predicts effects in identity-contested subcategories where patients retain substitution autonomy (estrogens and contraceptives) and a null for androgens, where physician-controlled prescribing removes patient substitution autonomy.
